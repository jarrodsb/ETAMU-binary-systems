# Research Log: 2026-07-27

## Goal

- Explore training a model on a dataset of multi-planet systems
- Improve my dataset generation script to reduce class imbalance

## What I did

- With the help of Claude and Gemini, I updated my Python script for generating an s-type binary dataset, to alleviate the class imbalance issue.
- Apart from adding new explicit checks to the initial conditions of my systems such as overly eccentric orbits or insufficient Hill separation, the most substantial (and drastic) change that I made to my script was to set the binary semimajor axis from 1 AU to 10 AU. I had to update the orbital period global parameter from a simple 2*Pi, to the equation for the orbital period based on the mass and the semimajor axis, from Kepler's 3rd law.
- I ran the script for roughly 44 hours on my local machine to produce a new dataset with 24 extracted features, including system-wide, planet-specific, and planet-planet features. The dataset also boasts significantly improved class balance.
- I trained my RFC model on the new dataset and ran the performance metrics, and found that my model now significantly outperforms both my earlier version that was trained on single-planet systems, as well as the rho_crit_HW99 analytic baseline.

## Results

- As a result of my more spread-out planetary systems, the long integrations of my script have resulted in dramatically more systems that are stable over 10,000 orbits, at the cost of significantly increased integration time required per system.
- I have a new dataset of 10,000 2-planet systems. 5,311 are listed as stable, and 4689 are listed as unstable.
- According to performance metrics, my newly-trained RFC model achieves a ROC AUC score of 0.9679 and a PR score of 0.97.

## Problems or questions

- The dataset generation needed a very long time to run, likely as a result of updating the orbital period for integrations to account for the change to a 10 AU binary semimajor axis.
- I am a little suspicious of the fact that my dataset now contains so many "stable" systems, after experiencing such an extreme class imbalance previously.
